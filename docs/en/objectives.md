# VIDA research objectives

English translation of `docs/objetivos.md` (Spanish original is canonical).

Stated by Oziel Atlahi Montoya Mares (22 September 2026). They are the project's compass; every technical phase must be explainable as a step toward one of them.

| Level | Question |
|---|---|
| **Final research objective** | Can artificial consciousness exist? |
| **Subsequent objective** | If it exists, what constitutes its identity and continuity? |
| **Long-term objective** | Can that continuity be maintained when changing substrate? |
| **Extreme objective** | Could some form of indefinite preservation of a conscious mind be achieved? |

## How they relate to the current work

- VIDA-0.1 (agents with memory, needs, relationships, learning and behavioural identity) builds the *experimental object* on which these questions can be formulated. It does not answer any of them yet.
- The scientific interpretation principle of the design document (section 37) remains in force: an observation ("the agent did X") is not an interpretation ("it is consistent with self-representation") nor a hypothesis ("a self-representation mechanism might exist"). None of the three demonstrates consciousness. For that reason the code, the logs and the biographies describe behaviours and never assert conscious states.
- Planned order of approach: experience → memory → learning → identity → continuity → transfer → preservation (section 45). The continuity and transfer experiments (phases 4 and 5) are the first point at which the second and third objectives become measurable with the Individual Continuity Index.
- The first objective requires, before anything else, operational criteria: which observations would count as evidence for or against. Defining them is pending work, prior to any experiment on consciousness.
